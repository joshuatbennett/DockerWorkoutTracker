# 4-Day Upper/Lower Strength & Muscle Program
### Home Gym Edition — Barbell, Power Cage, Adjustable Bench, Cable Pulley

---

## How This Works

This is an **Upper/Lower split** run 4 days a week. It hits every muscle group twice weekly (the sweet spot for intermediate muscle/strength gains), alternates a **strength-focused day** and a **hypertrophy-focused day** for each half of the body, and keeps sessions to about 60 minutes.

**Weekly layout:**
| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Upper A (Strength) |
| Day 2 | Lower A (Strength) |
| Day 3 | Rest |
| Day 4 | Upper B (Hypertrophy) |
| Day 5 | Lower B (Hypertrophy) |
| Day 6-7 | Rest |

**Only training 3x/week?** Rotate through Upper A → Lower A → Upper B → Lower B → Upper A... one session per training day, just keep moving through the list in order regardless of the calendar. Each muscle group still gets hit roughly every 5-9 days, which is plenty for maintenance/slow growth — if pure size/strength is the main goal, 4 days will get you there faster.

**Cable pulley notes:** Your 3 fixed positions map like this:
- **Low** (bottom of upright) → rows, curls, pull-throughs, low-to-high raises
- **Waist height** → chest press, woodchops, some lateral raise variations
- **High** (top of cage) → pulldowns, pushdowns, face pulls, straight-arm pulldowns

## Warm-Up (every session, ~5-8 min)
- 5 min light cardio or jumping jacks to raise heart rate
- Dynamic stretches for the muscles you're training (arm circles, leg swings, hip openers)
- 2 warm-up sets of your first exercise: bar/empty-ish weight for 10 reps, then a moderate weight for 5 reps before your working sets

## Progression: How to Add Weight

Use **double progression**:
1. Pick a weight you can do for the *bottom* of the rep range with good form.
2. Each session, try to add 1-2 reps to each set.
3. Once you hit the *top* of the rep range for all sets, add weight next session (2.5-5kg / 5-10lb for upper body lifts, 5-10kg / 10-20lb for lower body lifts) and drop back to the bottom of the rep range.

For the strength-day compound lifts (squat, bench, row, OHP), you can also just try to add a small amount of weight every session as long as reps/form hold — this is normal for intermediates on the main lifts and will naturally slow down over time.

## What To Do When You Miss Reps

The **scheduled deload** (every 5-6 weeks at ~60%) is separate from what to do when a specific lift stalls in the moment. Here's how to handle that:

- **Missed reps once:** No action needed. Just attempt the same weight again next session — one off day is normal (sleep, stress, fatigue all play a part).
- **Missed the same weight 2-3 sessions in a row:** This is a real stall. Cut the weight ~10% and build back up over the next couple of weeks — this is the standard fix and it almost always works.
- **Multiple lifts stalling at once:** This usually means accumulated fatigue is the real issue. Take your scheduled deload week now instead of waiting for the calendar, then resume.
- **Rule of thumb:** 3 failed attempts at a weight = stop grinding at it and back off. Don't fight for ugly reps just to hit a number — that's how injuries happen, and a controlled step back is always faster than an injury layoff.

## A Few Notes
- **Landmine Press setup**: wedge one end of the empty barbell firmly into a wall corner (or a rolled towel/sandbag in a corner for grip). Load the other end with plates. Stand facing the loaded end, hold it at shoulder height with both hands (or one hand at a time), and press up and slightly forward at an angle — the diagonal bar path is what keeps it clear of a low ceiling. Alternate which foot is forward each set for balance, or do it half-kneeling for a more stable base.
- **Pulldowns instead of pull-ups**: Day 1 and Day 4 both use the lat pulldown now, but at different rep ranges (6-8 heavy on Day 1, 8-10 lighter on Day 4) so they still train slightly differently. As your pulldown strength builds, try a couple of pull-up reps every few weeks as a bonus set at the end of Day 1 — once you can comfortably do sets of 5+, feel free to swap pull-ups back in on Day 1 and keep pulldowns on Day 4.
- **No dumbbells** means unilateral/dumbbell-specific movements are swapped for barbell or cable equivalents above — this is intentional, not a compromise.
- **Deload**: every 5-6 weeks, take one week at ~60% of your normal weights to let joints and CNS recover, then jump back in.
- **Track your lifts** — writing down weights/reps each session is what actually drives progress; it's easy to plateau without it.
- If any exercise doesn't feel right for your joints or setup, swap it for a similar movement pattern (e.g., swap Bulgarian split squats for regular walking lunges) rather than skipping the muscle group entirely.
